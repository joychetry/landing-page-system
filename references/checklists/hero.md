# Checklist: hero (above the fold)

Used in step 8 (writing the hero copy), steps 9 and 11 (laying it out) and step 14 (last checks). The hero carries most of the page: an industry rule of thumb is that around 60% of visitors never scroll past it, so everything below must be true before the page moves on.

## Headline: the dream outcome

- [ ] The headline states the end result the customer wants, not who you are or what you do.
- [ ] The end result is quantified where an honest number exists (and that number is in research/04-proof.md or research/01-goal-offer.md).
- [ ] A time period appears only if it is true and typical for customers; otherwise it is left out.
- [ ] The headline carries an emotional payoff (how life feels once the result arrives), in words from the language bank in research/02-customers.md where possible.
- [ ] A stranger who reads only the headline for 5 seconds can say what they would get.
- [ ] The headline is 14 words or fewer.
- [ ] The headline does not open with the company name, "Welcome to", "We are" or a category label.
- [ ] The headline could not be pasted unchanged onto a direct competitor's page (checked against research/03-competitors.md).
- [ ] The headline is the page's only H1 and includes the primary keyword or a close variant, worked in without bending the promise to fit it.

## Subheadline: how you deliver it

- [ ] The subheadline explains how the headline promise is achieved.
- [ ] It opens with a pain hook or pattern interrupt ("Tired of X?", "Stop doing X") taken from real customer complaints.
- [ ] It names the 3 to 4 biggest value props, each one a facet from the offer breakdown in research/01-goal-offer.md.
- [ ] The value props named are related to the headline, not a random feature list.
- [ ] It reads in one breath: two sentences at most.

## Proof above the fold

- [ ] Exactly two proof items are visible in the hero (for example a rating with review count, a customer count, a named testimonial, press logos, a result stat, before and after).
- [ ] The two proof items are different types, not two versions of the same claim.
- [ ] Each proof item traces to a row in research/04-proof.md with a source and permission status.
- [ ] No proof is invented, rounded up or borrowed from another product. If proof is missing, it is listed under Gaps in research/04-proof.md and the slot is redesigned, not faked.
- [ ] For a visual offer, the proof is visual (before and after, real photos) rather than a stat.

## One call to action

- [ ] There is exactly one primary CTA button in the hero.
- [ ] The button label says what they get ("Get my free quote", "Start my 14-day trial"), not "Submit", "Learn more" or "Click here".
- [ ] The CTA matches the single conversion action in research/01-goal-offer.md.
- [ ] Any secondary action (phone number, "see how it works" link) is visually quieter than the primary button.
- [ ] The same CTA wording or action is repeated in the closer at the bottom of the page.

## FUD reducers

- [ ] One to three short reducers sit next to or under the CTA (guarantee, free cancellation, no card required, warranty, response time).
- [ ] Every reducer is real and confirmed by the owner. No guarantee, trial or policy is invented to fill the slot.
- [ ] Each reducer answers a specific objection found in research/02-customers.md.

## Reinforcing visual

- [ ] The hero image or video shows either the thing they get (product, screenshot, deliverable) or the outcome they want.
- [ ] The visual proves something the headline or subhead claims; it is not decorative stock.
- [ ] It uses real product, team or customer imagery from assets/ or brand/assets/, not a placeholder.
- [ ] Video, if used, does not autoplay with sound and has a poster image.

## Fits above the fold

- [ ] At 1280px wide, headline, subhead, both proof items, the CTA, FUD reducers and the visual are all visible without scrolling.
- [ ] At 360px wide, headline, subhead, both proof items, the CTA and FUD reducers are visible without scrolling, with the elements stacked; the visual is at least partly in view.
- [ ] Nothing in the hero (cookie banner, chat widget, sticky bar) covers the CTA on first load at either width.
- [ ] The headline is the largest text on the screen and the CTA is the highest-contrast element.

## Section 2 (directly after the hero)

- [ ] Section 2 was chosen by traffic and offer: social proof first for warm, high-intent traffic (search ads, branded or "near me" searches); pain point first for cold, interruption traffic (social ads) or complex and B2B offers.
- [ ] The choice and the reason are written in pages/<page>/07-structure.md.
- [ ] Social proof version: a visible wall of reviews, stacked one by one on mobile, never a carousel (rule of thumb: only 1 to 2% of visitors click past the first slide).
- [ ] Pain point version: a before and after visual if the offer can be shown; otherwise long-form problem, agitate, solution copy.

## Proof and pain headlines

- [ ] Every proof or pain section headline leads with the benefit the reader gets ("Join 2,000 homeowners who cut their energy bill"), using only true numbers.
- [ ] No proof headline reads "Our testimonials", "Our reviews", "See what our customers say", "What our clients say" or "Real people, real results".
- [ ] Each proof or pain headline makes sense to a reader who skips the body copy beneath it.
